# Tabs

`<craft-tabs>` is a strip of triggers over a set of panels, one visible at a time.

<Canvas of={TabsStories.Default} sourceState="shown" />

Use tabs for alternative views of the same subject — an entry's content, its settings, its metadata. Not for steps in a sequence, which is a wizard, and not for content a person needs to compare, which they cannot do when only one panel is visible.

## Pairing

Tabs and panels are slotted as **siblings**, paired by document order: the first `slot="tab"` owns the first `slot="panel"`.

```html
<craft-tabs label="Entry settings">
  <craft-tab slot="tab">Content</craft-tab>
  <div slot="panel">…</div>
  <craft-tab slot="tab">Settings</craft-tab>
  <div slot="panel">…</div>
</craft-tabs>
```

That looks unusual next to a nested API, and it is deliberate: because a panel is not wrapped in anything this component defines, it can be a `<div>`, a [Pane](?path=/docs/components-pane--docs), or a form section, with no wrapper in the way.

A mismatched count logs a warning and leaves the extras inert.

## Overflow

A strip too narrow for its tabs collapses the excess into a menu rather than wrapping or scrolling silently:

<Canvas of={TabsStories.Overflow} sourceState="shown" />

<Canvas of={TabsStories.NoOverflow} sourceState="shown" />

Or turn the problem around with `equal-width`, which divides the strip evenly between the tabs so they shrink to fit instead of collapsing:

<Canvas of={TabsStories.EqualWidth} sourceState="shown" />

The two are alternatives rather than layers. `equal-width` turns the overflow measurement off, because it makes a tab's width a fact about the container instead of about its label — there is nothing left to measure, and nothing that needs collapsing.

## External Panels

A panel doesn't have to be slotted into the strip. A [Tab](?path=/docs/components-tab--docs) can name its panel by id with `controls`, and the strip wires the two together across the document instead of by position.

<Canvas of={TabsStories.ExternalPanels} sourceState="shown" />

To follow the WAI-ARIA APG tabs pattern, an external panel still has to be next in the DOM: place the panels immediately after `<craft-tabs>`, with no content between them and the tablist. Screen reader users read from the tabs straight into the selected panel, and <kbd>Tab</kbd> moves from the selected tab into it.

If the triggers and the content need to live in different parts of the page, such as buttons in a toolbar and panels in a pane, they aren't tabs. Use a [Disclosure](?path=/docs/components-disclosure--docs) for each one instead.

## Accessibility

Lion supplies the whole tab contract: the `role`, `aria-controls`, and `aria-labelledby` wiring, the roving tabindex, and arrow/Home/End navigation.

It follows the APG [Tabs with Automatic Activation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/tabs/examples/tabs-automatic/) pattern: the arrow keys move focus and selection together.

Three things stay with you:

- **Name the strip.** `label` is required: set it to what the tabs switch between, such as "Entry settings". A strip without one logs an error, and the PHP `Tabs` component throws. The tablist is in the shadow root, so `aria-labelledby` on the host can't reach it.
- **Keep labels short.** They sit in a strip, and a long label is what pushes a tab into the overflow menu.
- **Do not use tabs to hide required form fields.** A validation error in a panel nobody has opened is an error nobody can find.
